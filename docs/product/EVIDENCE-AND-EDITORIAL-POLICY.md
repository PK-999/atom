# ATOM Evidence and Editorial Policy

## 1. Purpose

ATOM's credibility depends on showing evidence fairly, including inconvenient evidence.

This document defines how quantitative data, studies, claims, uncertainty, and editorial language should be handled.

---

## 2. Source Priority

### Tier A — Primary / institutional / scientific
Prefer:
- IAEA
- UNSCEAR
- IPCC
- IEA
- OECD NEA
- WHO
- World Bank
- national nuclear regulators
- national statistical agencies
- peer-reviewed research
- primary datasets

### Tier B — High-quality aggregators
May include:
- Our World in Data
- other transparent evidence aggregators

### Tier C — Contextual / interested parties
May include:
- industry associations
- environmental NGOs
- advocacy organizations
- think tanks
- news media

Tier C is allowed, but perspective and conflicts of interest should be considered.

---

## 3. Source Selection Rules

Prefer sources that are:
- methodologically transparent
- current enough for the claim
- primary where practical
- reproducible
- clearly scoped
- relevant to geography/technology
- appropriately licensed

Do not choose a source solely because it supports a preferred conclusion.

---

## 4. Quantitative Claim Requirements

Every important number should capture where applicable:
- source
- publication year
- data year
- geography
- technology
- unit
- system boundary
- method
- transformation
- uncertainty/range
- last verified

Never strip numbers from their context.

---

## 5. Representative Values

A representative value may be:
- median
- mean
- central estimate
- model default
- regulator-reported value

The UI must state which.

Do not present a single number as universal if the evidence is a distribution.

---

## 6. Uncertainty

Uncertainty can come from:
- measurement
- modeling
- study design
- system boundary
- geography
- technology
- future assumptions
- discount rates
- policy assumptions
- incomplete records

ATOM should explain the source of uncertainty where useful.

Do not fabricate numeric confidence scores.

---

## 7. Disagreement

When credible sources disagree:

Present:
1. the claim
2. supporting evidence
3. challenging evidence
4. main methodological reasons for disagreement
5. where there is broad agreement
6. what remains uncertain

Do not use false balance.
Do not manufacture controversy where strong scientific consensus exists.

---

## 8. Accident Coverage

Accidents must not be minimized or sensationalized.

Cover:
- immediate causes
- technical failures
- institutional failures
- radiation releases
- health impacts
- evacuation/displacement
- environmental impacts
- uncertainty
- regulatory changes
- design changes

Distinguish:
- direct radiation mortality
- accident mortality
- evacuation-related mortality
- long-term modeled risk
- confirmed epidemiological effects

---

## 9. Radiation Coverage

Always distinguish:
- activity
- absorbed dose
- equivalent/effective dose
- dose rate
- contamination
- irradiation

Analogies must not erase scientific meaning.

---

## 10. Economics Coverage

Avoid a single universal statement such as:
> nuclear is cheap

or:
> nuclear is expensive

Separate:
- capital cost
- financing cost
- operating cost
- fuel cost
- construction duration
- cost overruns
- plant lifetime
- capacity factor
- system integration
- market structure
- country/project context

---

## 11. Climate Coverage

Lifecycle emissions must define:
- lifecycle boundary
- technology
- geography where relevant
- central value or distribution
- source methodology

Do not equate operational emissions with lifecycle emissions.

---

## 12. Waste Coverage

Distinguish:
- low-level waste
- intermediate-level waste
- high-level waste
- spent fuel
- reprocessing
- storage
- disposal
- geological repositories

Do not use vague phrases like “nuclear waste lasts forever.”

Explain timescales and hazard evolution precisely.

---

## 13. Proliferation

Avoid both extremes:
- civilian nuclear power has no proliferation connection
- civilian nuclear power automatically leads to weapons

Explain:
- enrichment
- reprocessing
- plutonium
- safeguards
- fuel-cycle choices
- institutional controls

---

## 14. Editorial Tone

Prefer:
- “evidence suggests”
- “under this methodology”
- “historically”
- “estimates vary”
- “this scenario assumes”
- “the strongest evidence supports”

Avoid:
- “obviously”
- “everyone knows”
- “completely safe”
- “zero risk”
- “perfect”
- “disaster-proof”
- “renewables cannot work”
- “nuclear solves climate change”

---

## 15. Correction Policy

When an error is found:
- correct the value/content
- record source/version change
- update last-verified metadata
- consider a visible correction note for material public errors

Do not silently rewrite major disputed claims without traceability.

---

## 16. AI Assistant Rule

Ask ATOM may explain evidence but must not invent evidence.

If retrieval is insufficient:
> I do not have enough reliable evidence in the ATOM library to answer this confidently.

Every substantive factual answer should link back to evidence.
